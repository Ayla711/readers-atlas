# Eastern first-batch fragment

10 books, 24 places, 20 draft lines (19 verbatim public-domain excerpts and one original Frost scene), four required pilgrimage photographs. `build.py` regenerates all JSON from the actual downloaded Gutenberg byte files; `extraction-specs.json` records start/end anchors, `evidence.json` records offsets, hashes, coordinate fields, author biographies and Commons licences.

Coverage: CT and ME through Stowe homes linked to the central fragment's `uncle-toms-cabin`; RI through the Providence Athenaeum's documented Poe association; VT through Frost's Stone House; DE/DC/MD/MA/NH/NJ/NY/NC/OH/PA/SC/VA/WV through excerpts, prototypes or Frost's scene. Every state association is independently checked against final Census boundaries by the root validator.

The nine public-domain titles use downloaded Gutenberg originals; Robert Frost died in 1963 and his New Hampshire collection receives only a newly written bilingual scene summary. All dates are checked against fetched author biography text. His available Gutenberg text was used to understand the Franconia incident, not quoted in the pack.

Two authors are outside the supplied canon: George Alfred Townsend's The Entailed Hat supplies Delaware; the Delaware Public Archives study `https://archivesfiles.delaware.gov/ebooks/GATHs_Literary_Work_and_Folk.pdf` discusses the novel's regional literary significance. Charles W. Chesnutt's The Marrow of Tradition supplies North Carolina, with the state cultural agency's public writer memorial as selection basis: `https://www.dncr.nc.gov/blog/2023/12/29/charles-w-chesnutt-1858-1932-i-32`. The Wellington/Wilmington association is separately sourced. Wolfe was deferred because a usable text satisfying the required Gutenberg download path was not established.

Orchard House is an explicit prototype, supported by the museum's own statement that Alcott wrote and set Little Women there. Clyde is described as an influence on fictional Winesburg, not identified with the separate real town called Winesburg. Great Neck represents West Egg at community scale. The West Virginia excerpt concerns the Northern Panhandle region: Weirton is an inland representative point, not an asserted Whitman stop or precise railway route.

All four pilgrimage entries distinguish author association from fictional scene setting and include dated historical context/current use. Brunswick was rented in 1850–1852, as confirmed by Bowdoin College's official page `https://www.bowdoin.edu/english/stowe-house/index.html`; only the writing room has scheduled public access, while the house also contains offices. The Providence library was visited by Poe in 1848, not his home. Hartford is Stowe's later 1873–1896 residence, not where she wrote Uncle Tom's Cabin. Frost's residence dates are 1920–1929.

Independent western review corrected the source headings for usmt-1 (Chapter 16), useh-3 (Chapter 24) and useh-4 (Chapter 21). Root review expanded three clipped extracts to complete sentences, preserving all original CRLF and punctuation. The Gatsby Plaza passage now includes Jordan's surrounding sentence, not only its parenthesis. Chinese translations were updated to match. The Brunswick photo is explicitly labelled a historical postcard from about 1905, not the present exterior.

All four actual Commons file-page HTML caches are present as photo0.html–photo3.html. Licences: CC BY 4.0, Public domain, CC BY 4.0, CC BY-SA 3.0. No image binaries downloaded. All metadata and translations remain draft for project-owner review. No second batch generated.
